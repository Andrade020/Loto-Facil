\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The classical literature on lotto designs \cite{Furedi1996, Li2002,
Gordon1995} seeks the minimum number of tickets required to guarantee a
prize.  Our probabilistic framework addresses a complementary question:
given exactly $n$ tickets (typically far fewer than the deterministic
minimum), how should they be arranged to maximise the probability of
winning?  The two objectives align when $n \ge C(v,k,t)$, but for practical
budgets they lead to different optimisation problems.  Theorem~\ref{thm:two-ticket-monotone}
and Proposition~\ref{prop:mutual-excl} show that the probabilistic problem
has a cleaner structure in the high-$k$ regime: at near-minimum overlap the
two winning events are mutually exclusive, so $P_{\mathrm{win}} = 2p$
exactly.  No analogous result appears in the deterministic covering literature.

\citet{Liu2024} study a related probabilistic formulation for the UK 6/59
lottery and find that spreading pairwise overlap \emph{evenly} across the
ticket pairs --- balanced overlap --- is what improves the probability of
winning smaller prizes, reporting gains of three to four percentage points
over uniform random selection.  Our results give this phenomenon an exact
mechanism, in a single-tier setting and a very different parameter regime.
For $n = 2$, minimum overlap is optimal
(Theorem~\ref{thm:two-ticket-monotone}), but from $n = 3$ onwards the
degree bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-bound} makes all-minimum
profiles infeasible, and the convexity of the joint-win function $g$ makes
the \emph{balanced} profile optimal --- certified exactly for $n = 3$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:three-optimal}, where the optimum $\{7,7,6\}$ contains no
minimum-overlap pair at all.  Balancedness is therefore not a multi-tier
artefact: it already governs the single-tier problem whenever more than
two tickets are in play.

The framework applies to any $(v,k)$-lottery with a single prize threshold.
Three natural extensions illustrate the range of parameters.  In Lotto 6/49
(Canada, Germany), $v=49$, $k=6$, $m^*=0$: tickets can be disjoint, and by
Proposition~\ref{prop:mutual-excl} disjoint tickets have mutually exclusive
winning events precisely when $2t > k$ --- true for the thresholds
$t \in \{4,5,6\}$, but false at $t = 3 = k/2$, where a draw can split three
matches and three matches across two disjoint tickets.  The same dichotomy
applies to the Brazilian Mega-Sena ($v=60$, $k=6$, exclusivity for every
prize tier $t \ge 4$) and Quina ($v=80$, $k=5$, exclusivity for $t \ge 3$).
The Lotof\'{a}cil is distinguished by its ratio $k/v = 15/25 = 0.6$, which
forces non-trivial overlap ($m^* = 5$) and makes the optimisation problem
genuinely combinatorial.

\begin{remark}[Expected value]
  In a model with a fixed prize $V$ and ticket cost $c$, the expected return
  of an $n$-ticket collection is $V \cdot P_{\mathrm{win}}(\mathcal{A}) - nc$.
  Because $V$ and $nc$ are constants, maximising $P_{\mathrm{win}}$ is
  equivalent to maximising expected return: our method reduces the expected
  loss for a given budget, even though it cannot make expected return positive.
  The Lotof\'{a}cil's actual return rate is approximately 45\% of revenue
  \citep{CEF2024}, so the expected return on a single ticket of cost~$c$ is
  roughly $-0.55c$, regardless of the numbers chosen.  In the real game,
  prizes at each tier are pooled and divided among all winners, so the
  expected return also depends on how many other players hold the same
  winning combination.  Our method addresses only the $P_{\mathrm{win}}$
  component; the prize-division component would require data on the
  distribution of bets across all players, which we do not use.
\end{remark}

There are three main limitations to the current analysis.  First, the
model ignores prize division.  In practice, a ticket that avoids popular
number combinations earns a higher expected payout even with the same
prize probability, because fewer other players hold the same ticket.
Incorporating this would require data on the distribution of all bets
placed, which we do not use.  Second, although the monotonicity question
is now settled --- best-realisation monotonicity is proved for $n = 3$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-monotone}) and refuted for $n = 4$
(Example~\ref{ex:n4-counterexample}) --- the \emph{balance principle}
that emerges (global optima attain the degree bound of
Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-bound} with a $\sum g$-minimising profile)
is certified only where exhaustive enumeration is possible: the
Lotof\'{a}cil with $3 \le n \le 6$ and the small $n = 4$ families of
Remark~\ref{rem:balance-principle}.  Its general validity is the natural
open problem left by this work.  Third, exact optimality for the
Lotof\'{a}cil is certified up to $n = 6$
(Theorems~\ref{thm:three-optimal} and~\ref{thm:certified-optima}); for
$n \ge 7$ the balanced profiles produced by minimising $G$ are supported
by the degree bound and by the empirical results, but a certificate of
global optimality is open.  The obstruction is the profile search, whose
cost grows as $11^{\binom{n}{2}}$ before pruning: the certified cases
required $2.6 \times 10^4$, $1.9 \times 10^7$ and $2.8 \times 10^{11}$
nodes for $n = 4, 5, 6$, so $n = 7$ would need a sharper bound than the
third-order truncation \eqref{eq:bonferroni-ub} rather than merely more
computing time.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied the selection of $n$ tickets in a $(v,k)$-lottery to maximise
the probability that at least one ticket wins.  Four contributions stand
out.  First, for $n=2$ we proved --- for every prize threshold $t$, via a
measure-preserving swap coupling --- that the joint prize probability is
non-increasing in pairwise overlap (Theorem~\ref{thm:two-ticket-monotone}),
with an exact closed form for the increment; tickets with overlap at most
$2t-k-1$ (six, for the Lotof\'{a}cil) have mutually exclusive winning
events and achieve $P_{\mathrm{win}} = 2p$ exactly
(Proposition~\ref{prop:mutual-excl}).  Second, we showed that for
$n \ge 3$ the naive extension of this monotonicity fails: $P_{\mathrm{win}}$
is not determined by pairwise overlaps, coordinatewise monotonicity admits
explicit counterexamples, all-minimum-overlap profiles are combinatorially
infeasible, and the exact optimum for three Lotof\'{a}cil tickets is the
\emph{balanced} profile $\{7,7,6\}$ with
$P_{\mathrm{win}} = 3p - 2g(7) \approx 0.3147$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:three-optimal}).  A certified branch-and-bound in exact
integer arithmetic extends this to four, five and six tickets
(Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-optima}): the optima are the balanced
profiles $\{8^3,7^3\}$, $\{8^5,7^5\}$ and $\{8^{15}\}$, each attaining
the degree bound with equality and beating uncoordinated play by $4.4$,
$6.9$ and $7.4$ percentage points.  The monotonicity question is settled
in full --- best-realisation monotonicity holds for $n = 3$, proved via a
pattern-preserving swap coupling (Lemma~\ref{lem:pattern-swap},
Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-monotone}), and fails from $n = 4$ onwards ---
while every certified global optimum attains the degree bound with a
balanced profile.  Third, we established that uncoordinated random play achieves the
independence benchmark $U_n$ exactly in expectation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:random-benchmark}), which turns every gain over the
random baseline into a gain over independence itself.  Fourth, two
algorithms (Algorithms~\ref{alg:greedy} and~\ref{alg:sa}, the latter
minimising the convex joint-win loss $G = \sum_{i<j} g(m_{ij})$) recover
the certified optima where they are known, and a paired Monte Carlo
experiment on the Lotof\'{a}cil shows absolute gains over random selection
of up to $10.4$ percentage points at $n = 10$, statistically significant
at every $n \ge 2$, with saturation ($P_{\mathrm{win}} \ge 0.999$) reached
at roughly half the budget required by random play ($n = 40$ versus
$n = 75$).  To our knowledge, these results provide the first rigorous
probabilistic treatment of multi-ticket lottery selection in the high-$k$
regime; they lay groundwork for extensions to multi-tier prize structures
and prize-division models.

\paragraph*{Reproducibility and code availability.}
All exact results (Tables~\ref{tab:delta-g}, \ref{tab:three-profiles}
and~\ref{tab:certified-optima}, Theorems~\ref{thm:three-optimal},
\ref{thm:certified-optima} and~\ref{thm:profile-monotone}, and the
counterexamples) are computed in exact integer arithmetic; every Monte
Carlo experiment uses a fixed, reported seed.  The certificates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-optima} are produced by two independent
implementations --- one in Python, one in Julia, written from the same
specification but sharing no code --- which agree on every value, and
each certified optimal collection is additionally verified by brute-force
enumeration of all $\binom{25}{15} = 3{,}268{,}760$ draws.  The complete
source code --- the optimiser, the benchmark pipeline, and a verification
suite that independently reproduces each table, certificate, and
counterexample --- is provided as supplementary material.

\section{Computation of Table~\ref{tab:delta-g}}
\label{sec:appendix-computation}

The values $g(m)$ in Table~\ref{tab:delta-g} were computed by exact
enumeration of the multivariate hypergeometric distribution.  For parameters
$v=25$, $k=15$, $t=11$ and overlap $m$, the partition sizes are
$(|S_0|,|S_1|,|S_2|,|S_3|) = (m, 15{-}m, 15{-}m, m{-}5)$.  The joint-win
probability is
\[
  g(m) = \frac{1}{\binom{25}{15}}
  \sum_{\substack{x_0+x_1+x_2+x_3=15 \\
        x_i \le |S_i| \\
        x_0+x_1 \ge 11,\; x_0+x_2 \ge 11}}
  \binom{m}{x_0}\binom{15{-}m}{x_1}\binom{15{-}m}{x_2}\binom{m{-}5}{x_3}.
\]
All arithmetic used Python's \texttt{math.comb} for exact integer
evaluation; no floating-point rounding occurs until the final division
by $\binom{25}{15} = 3{,}268{,}760$.

The supplementary verification scripts recompute Table~\ref{tab:delta-g}
and the increment formula \eqref{eq:increment} (2{,}303 parameter cases,
exact arithmetic), enumerate all $1{,}250$ feasible three-ticket profiles
behind Theorem~\ref{thm:three-optimal}, verify the counterexample of
Example~\ref{ex:counterexample} by brute force over all
$\binom{11}{7} = 330$ draws, validate the identity of
Lemma~\ref{lem:pattern-swap} on several hundred exact instances with
$n \in \{3,4\}$, verify Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-monotone} exhaustively
for all $(v,k,t)$ with $v \le 13$, and establish the $n=4$
counterexamples of Example~\ref{ex:n4-counterexample} by complete
enumeration of all four-ticket collections in the stated families.

The certificates of Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-optima} are produced by a
self-contained Julia program that, for each $n \in \{4,5,6\}$, runs the
branch-and-bound of the proof, enumerates the admissible intersection
structures of every surviving profile, reports the optimal profiles and
their multiplicities, and cross-validates the resulting collection by
brute force over all $\binom{25}{15}$ draws.  The program reproduces the
independently computed $n = 4$ certificate as a regression test before
each run.  The searches complete in seconds for $n = 4,5$; the $n = 6$
search visited $2.8 \times 10^{11}$ nodes and took $13.5$ hours on ten
cores.
