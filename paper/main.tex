%% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
We study the selection of $n$ tickets in a $(v,k)$-lottery: each ticket is
a $k$-subset of $[v]$, a draw is a uniformly random $k$-subset, and a
prize is paid for at least $t$ matches.  The objective is to maximise the
probability that at least one ticket wins.  For two tickets we prove, for
every threshold $t$, that this probability is non-increasing in the
overlap $|A_1 \cap A_2|$, via a measure-preserving swap coupling that also
yields a closed form for the increment; tickets with overlap at most
$2t-k-1$ have mutually exclusive winning events.  For more tickets the
picture changes qualitatively: the winning probability is not a function
of the pairwise overlaps, all-minimum-overlap collections are infeasible
by a degree-counting bound, and optimal collections are \emph{balanced}.
We determine the exact optima for the Brazilian Lotof\'{a}cil
($v=25$, $k=15$, $t=11$) with three to six tickets --- the balanced
overlap profiles $\{7,7,6\}$, $\{8^3,7^3\}$, $\{8^5,7^5\}$ and
$\{8^{15}\}$, certified by exhaustive enumeration and by branch-and-bound
in exact integer arithmetic --- and resolve the monotonicity question:
monotonicity of the
best-realisation value function holds for $n = 3$, which we prove, and
fails from $n = 4$ onwards, for which we exhibit counterexamples.  We
further show that i.i.d.\ random play attains the independence benchmark
$1-(1-p)^n$ exactly in expectation, so outperforming the random baseline
means outperforming independence.  A greedy coverage stage followed by
simulated annealing on the convex pairwise joint-win loss recovers the
certified optima and, in a paired Monte Carlo study of the Lotof\'{a}cil
with $n$ up to $100$, yields absolute gains over random play of up to
$10.4$ percentage points, reaching near-certainty of a prize at roughly
half the budget that random play requires.
\end{abstract}
