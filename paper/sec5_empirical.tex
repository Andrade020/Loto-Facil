\section{Empirical Study: The Brazilian Lotof\'{a}cil}
\label{sec:empirical}

The \emph{Lotof\'{a}cil} (``Easy Lotto'') is a fixed-odds lottery operated
by Caixa Econ\^{o}mica Federal, Brazil, since November 2003
\citep{CEF2024}.  On each draw,
fifteen distinct numbers are selected uniformly at random from
$[25] = \{1,\ldots,25\}$; a ticket consists of exactly fifteen numbers
chosen by the player.  Prizes are awarded for matching $t \in \{11,12,13,
14,15\}$ numbers.  We study the lowest prize level $t = 11$, which occurs
most frequently.  The relevant parameters are $v=25$, $k=15$, $t=11$,
giving single-ticket prize probability $p(25,15,11) \approx 0.10589$
(equation~\eqref{eq:pone-lotofacil}), minimum pairwise overlap $m^* = 5$,
and independence benchmark $U_n = 1-(0.89411)^n$, which by
Proposition~\ref{prop:random-benchmark} is exactly the expected performance
of random play.

\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{sec:exp-design}

Three ticket-selection strategies are compared.  The \emph{random baseline}
draws $n$ tickets independently from $\binom{[25]}{15}$ with no coordination.
The \emph{greedy} strategy runs Algorithm~\ref{alg:greedy} on a pool of
$M = \max(500, 20n)$ random candidates.  The \emph{greedy+SA} strategy
starts from the greedy output and runs Algorithm~\ref{alg:sa} with the
balanced-overlap objective $G(\mathcal{A}) = \sum_{i<j} g(m_{ij})$,
$N_{\mathrm{iter}} = \min(500 + 100n, 2000)$ iterations, initial temperature
$T_0 = 5 \times 10^{-3}$ (on the scale of the $g$-increments of
Table~\ref{tab:delta-g}), cooling factor $\alpha = 0.9993$, and the final
deterministic quench.

For each strategy and value of $n$, the joint prize probability
$P_{\mathrm{win}}(\mathcal{A})$ is estimated by counting the fraction of
$N_{\mathrm{sim}} = 50{,}000$ simulated draws for which at least one ticket
achieves eleven or more matches.  A key design choice is that the same
$50{,}000$ draws are used to evaluate all three strategies in the same trial.
This paired structure removes Monte Carlo variance from the comparison:
the within-trial difference $\hat{P}_{\mathrm{SA}} - \hat{P}_{\mathrm{rand}}$
reflects ticket quality only, not random fluctuation in the draws.

For each $n$, the full pipeline is replicated for $B$ independent trials,
where $B = 50$ for $n \le 5$, $B = 40$ for $n \in \{7,10\}$, and $B = 30$
for $n \ge 15$.  The values tested are
$n \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 75, 100\}$.
All simulations used Python~3.10 with NumPy's \texttt{default\_rng};
the code and the exact-verification scripts are provided as supplementary
material.

Several features of the results deserve comment.  At $n=1$, all three
strategies produce the same value, consistent with
Proposition~\ref{prop:n1}.  The random column tracks $U_n$ closely at every
$n$, exactly as Proposition~\ref{prop:random-benchmark} predicts.  The
absolute gain of the optimised strategy over random rises to a peak of
$10.4$ percentage points at $n = 10$ and remains above six points for
$5 \le n \le 20$.

The optimised values agree with the exact theory where the latter is
available.  At $n = 2$ the SA estimate $0.2120 \pm 0.0005$ matches the
exact optimum $2p \approx 0.21178$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:min-overlap-optimal});
at $n = 3$ the estimate $0.3144 \pm 0.0006$ matches the exact optimum
$0.31467$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:three-optimal}, and the SA collections
realise the optimal profile $\{7,7,6\}$ in every trial (mean pairwise
overlap $6.667 = 20/3$).  The comparison extends to every certified case:
running the algorithm from ten independent seeds and evaluating each
output exactly by enumeration of all $\binom{25}{15}$ draws, the
certified optimum of Theorem~\ref{thm:certified-optima} is reached in
$10$, $3$, $9$ and $3$ of the ten runs for $n = 3,4,5,6$ respectively.
The algorithm therefore finds the global optimum in all four certified
cases, but not from every start: the landscape of $G$ contains suboptimal
balanced profiles in which the quench can settle.  In practice this
argues for wrapping Algorithm~\ref{alg:sa} in a multi-start loop and
keeping the best $G$ found, at a cost linear in the number of starts.
For larger $n$ the SA collections continue to attain
the degree bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-bound} with balanced
profiles: mean pairwise overlap exactly $7.5$ at $n=5$ and
$8.\overline{3}$ at $n=10$.

From $n \ge 2$ onwards the SA values \emph{exceed} the independence
benchmark $U_n$; at the peak, $P_{\mathrm{win}}^{\mathrm{SA}} = 0.778$
versus $U_{10} = 0.674$.  This is not a paradox: as established in
Section~\ref{sec:framework}, $U_n$ is not an upper bound, and coordinated
collections profit from the mutual-exclusivity mechanism of
Proposition~\ref{prop:mutual-excl}.  The gap between SA and the pure
greedy solution (up to $4.3$ points at $n = 15$) isolates the value of the
balanced-overlap objective: greedy fixes coverage, but only the $G$-driven
refinement fixes the overlap \emph{profile}.

\subsection{Saturation}
\label{sec:saturation}

To compare the optimised strategy against the independence benchmark we
define the efficiency ratio
$\eta(n) = P_{\mathrm{win}}^{\mathrm{SA}}(n) / U_n$.  Values above one
reflect the negative-correlation bonus; as $n$ grows, $\eta(n)$ converges
to one as both strategies approach $P_{\mathrm{win}} = 1$.  The empirical
saturation threshold, defined as the smallest tested $n$ for which
$P_{\mathrm{win}} \ge 0.999$, is $n = 40$ for the optimised strategy and
$n = 75$ for the random baseline: optimisation reaches near-certainty with
roughly half the budget.  Beyond $n = 50$ the absolute gain falls below
$0.4$ percentage points and further optimisation has negligible practical
value.
