\section{Overlap Monotonicity and Exact Optima}
\label{sec:theory}

We begin with three limiting cases that admit exact, closed-form results.
The main two-ticket monotonicity theorem follows, proved for every prize
threshold $t$ by a measure-preserving swap coupling, together with an exact
formula for the increment.  The final subsection develops the theory for
$n \ge 3$: an exact decomposition for three tickets, a degree-counting
feasibility bound, the exact optimum for three Lotof\'{a}cil tickets, and a
complete resolution of the monotonicity question --- naive coordinatewise
monotonicity fails already at $n = 3$, monotonicity of the
\emph{best-realisation} value function holds at $n = 3$ (we prove it) and
fails from $n = 4$ onwards (we exhibit counterexamples).

\begin{proposition}[Single-ticket trivial case]
  \label{prop:n1}
  For $n = 1$, $\Pwin(\{A_1\}) = \pone$ for every ticket $A_1$, and no
  selection strategy can improve upon this value.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Direct from Definition~\ref{def:prize}: $\pone$ depends only on $v$, $k$,
  $t$, not on the elements of $A_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Identical-ticket case]
  \label{prop:identical}
  If $A_1 = A_2 = \cdots = A_n$ (all tickets identical), then
  $\Pwin(\mathcal{A}) = \pone$ regardless of $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The events $\{|A_i \cap R| \ge t\}$ are identical for all $i$, so their
  union equals $\{|A_1 \cap R| \ge t\}$, which has probability $\pone$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Proposition~\ref{prop:identical} shows that a player who mistakenly
  purchases $n$ copies of the same ticket achieves no more probability of
  winning than a player who purchases a single ticket.  The $n$-fold
  investment yields zero probabilistic return.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Large-$n$ saturation]
  \label{prop:saturation}
  Fix $(v,k,t)$ with $1 \le t \le k \le v$.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item[(i)] For every $n \ge \Cvkt$ there exist collections of $n$
      tickets with $\Pwin = 1$; consequently
      $\max_{|\mathcal{A}| = n} \Pwin(\mathcal{A}) = 1$ for all such $n$.
    \item[(ii)] For i.i.d.\ uniform random collections,
      $E[\Pwin(\mathcal{A}^{(n)})] = 1 - (1-\pone)^n \to 1$ as
      $n \to \infty$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:random-benchmark}).
  \end{itemize}
  Convergence does \emph{not} hold for every sequence of collections: by
  Proposition~\ref{prop:identical}, identical-ticket collections have
  $\Pwin = \pone$ for every $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  (i) Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a covering design witnessing $\Cvkt$: every
  $t$-subset of $[v]$ is contained in at least one block of $\mathcal{C}$.
  For any draw $R$, pick a $t$-subset $T \subseteq R$ (possible since
  $t \le k$); the block $A \in \mathcal{C}$ with $T \subseteq A$ satisfies
  $|A \cap R| \ge |T| = t$.  Hence $\Pwin(\mathcal{C}) = 1$, and
  $\mathcal{C}$ padded with arbitrary further tickets works for every
  $n \ge \Cvkt$.  (ii) is immediate from
  Proposition~\ref{prop:random-benchmark} and $\pone > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Saturation is unavoidable]
  Saturation makes the optimisation problem interesting only for moderate
  $n$: once $n \ge \Cvkt$ an optimal strategy guarantees a prize with
  certainty, and even random strategies approach $\Pwin = 1$ exponentially
  fast in expectation.  Our empirical study (Section~\ref{sec:empirical})
  identifies the \emph{practical} saturation range of the Lotofácil: the
  optimised strategy exceeds $\Pwin = 0.999$ from $n \approx 40$, the
  random baseline only from $n \approx 75$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The two-ticket monotonicity theorem}
\label{sec:two-ticket}

We now prove the central theoretical result of the paper.  Recall from
Definition~\ref{def:partition} the canonical partition of $[v]$ associated
with two tickets $A_1, A_2$ with overlap $m = |A_1 \cap A_2|$.

\begin{theorem}[Two-ticket overlap monotonicity]
  \label{thm:two-ticket-monotone}
  Let $A_1, A_2 \in \binom{[v]}{k}$ and let $R$ be a uniformly random
  $k$-subset of $[v]$.  For any prize threshold $t$ with $1 \le t \le k$,
  the function
  \[
    m \;\longmapsto\; \Pwin(A_1, A_2)
    \;=\; P(|A_1 \cap R| \ge t \;\text{ or }\; |A_2 \cap R| \ge t)
  \]
  is non-increasing as a function of $m = |A_1 \cap A_2|$, ranging over
  $m^* = \max(0, 2k-v)$ to $m = k$.  Consequently, $\Pwin(A_1, A_2)$ is
  maximised at $m = m^*$ (not necessarily uniquely; see
  Corollary~\ref{cor:min-overlap-optimal}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By inclusion-exclusion,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ie}
    \Pwin(A_1, A_2) \;=\; 2\pone - g(m),
  \end{equation}
  where $g(m) = P(|A_1 \cap R| \ge t \text{ and } |A_2 \cap R| \ge t)$
  is the \emph{joint-win probability}.  Since $\pone$ does not depend on
  $m$, it suffices to show that $g(m+1) \ge g(m)$ for each
  $m \in \{m^*, \ldots, k-1\}$.

  Fix tickets $A_1, A_2$ with overlap $m < k$.  Choose any
  $x \in A_1 \setminus A_2$ and $y \in A_2 \setminus A_1$, and define the
  modified ticket $A_2' = (A_2 \setminus \{y\}) \cup \{x\}$.  Then
  $|A_2'| = k$ and $|A_1 \cap A_2'| = m + 1$.  Write $S = |A_1 \cap R|$,
  $W = |A_2 \cap R|$, and note
  $|A_2' \cap R| = W + \ind{x \in R} - \ind{y \in R}$, so that
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:delta}
    g(m+1) - g(m)
    = E\!\left[\ind{S \ge t}
       \left(\ind{W + \ind{x \in R} - \ind{y \in R} \ge t}
           - \ind{W \ge t}\right)\right].
  \end{equation}
  Decompose \eqref{eq:delta} according to the pair
  $(\ind{x \in R}, \ind{y \in R})$.  When $\ind{x \in R} = \ind{y \in R}$
  the two inner indicators coincide and the contribution vanishes.  On
  $\{x \in R,\, y \notin R\}$ the score of $A_2'$ is $W + 1$, so the inner
  difference is $\ind{W = t-1}$; on $\{x \notin R,\, y \in R\}$ it is
  $W - 1$, so the difference is $-\ind{W = t}$.  Hence
  \begin{align}
    g(m+1) - g(m)
    &= P(S \ge t,\, W = t-1,\, x \in R,\, y \notin R) \notag \\
    &\qquad - P(S \ge t,\, W = t,\, x \notin R,\, y \in R).
    \label{eq:delta2}
  \end{align}

  Now consider the \emph{swap map}
  $\varphi(R) = (R \setminus \{y\}) \cup \{x\}$, a bijection from
  $\{R \in \binom{[v]}{k} : y \in R,\, x \notin R\}$ onto
  $\{R \in \binom{[v]}{k}: x \in R,\, y \notin R\}$ that preserves the
  uniform measure.  Because $x \in A_1 \setminus A_2$ and
  $y \in A_2 \setminus A_1$, applying $\varphi$ increases $S$ by exactly
  one and decreases $W$ by exactly one.  Therefore $\varphi$ maps the event
  in the second term of \eqref{eq:delta2} bijectively onto
  $\{S \ge t+1,\, W = t-1,\, x \in R,\, y \notin R\}$, and
  \begin{align}
    g(m+1) - g(m)
    &= P(S \ge t,\, W = t-1,\, x \in R,\, y \notin R) \notag \\
    &\qquad - P(S \ge t+1,\, W = t-1,\, x \in R,\, y \notin R) \notag \\
    &= P(S = t,\, W = t-1,\, x \in R,\, y \notin R) \;\ge\; 0.
    \label{eq:delta3}
  \end{align}
  This holds for every $t$ with $1 \le t \le k$, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact increment formula]
  \label{cor:increment}
  Expanding the probability in \eqref{eq:delta3} over the refined partition
  of $[v]$ into $A_1 \cap A_2$, $(A_1 \setminus A_2)\setminus\{x\}$,
  $\{x\}$, $(A_2 \setminus A_1)\setminus\{y\}$, $\{y\}$, and
  $[v] \setminus (A_1 \cup A_2)$ yields the closed form
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:increment}
    g(m+1) - g(m)
    = \frac{1}{\binom{v}{k}} \sum_{j \ge 0}
      \binom{m}{j} \binom{k-m-1}{\,t-1-j\,}^{\!2}\,
      \binom{v-2k+m}{\,k+1-2t+j\,},
  \end{equation}
  with the convention that binomial coefficients with out-of-range
  arguments vanish: the event in \eqref{eq:delta3} requires $j$ draw
  elements in $A_1 \cap A_2$, the element $x$, exactly $t-1-j$ elements in
  each of the two private regions, and the remaining $k+1-2t+j$ elements
  outside $A_1 \cup A_2$.  In particular the increment vanishes exactly
  when the index range of \eqref{eq:increment} is empty, and the values in
  Table~\ref{tab:delta-g} are reproduced by \eqref{eq:increment}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Minimum overlap is optimal for $n=2$]
  \label{cor:min-overlap-optimal}
  Among all collections $\{A_1, A_2\} \in \binom{[v]}{k}^2$, the joint
  prize probability $\Pwin(A_1, A_2)$ is maximised when
  $|A_1 \cap A_2| = m^* = \max(0, 2k-v)$, with maximum value
  \[
    \Pwin^* \;=\; 2\pone - g(m^*).
  \]
  The maximiser need not be unique: $\Pwin(m) = \Pwin(m^*)$ for every $m$
  such that all increments \eqref{eq:increment} between $m^*$ and $m$
  vanish.  For the Lotofácil, $g(5) = g(6) = 0$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:mutual-excl}), so the maximum
  $\Pwin = 2\pone \approx 0.2118$ is attained exactly at $m \in \{5, 6\}$
  and $\Pwin$ decreases strictly from $m = 7$ onwards
  (Table~\ref{tab:delta-g}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Mutual exclusivity below the overlap threshold]
  \label{prop:mutual-excl}
  For any parameters $(v,k,t)$ and any two tickets with overlap
  $m = |A_1 \cap A_2| \le 2t - k - 1$, the joint-win event
  $\{|A_1 \cap R| \ge t\} \cap \{|A_2 \cap R| \ge t\}$ has probability
  \emph{zero}: the two winning events are mutually exclusive and
  $\Pwin(A_1, A_2) = 2\pone$ exactly.  For the Lotofácil
  $(v,k,t)=(25,15,11)$ the threshold is $2t-k-1 = 6$: two tickets sharing
  at most six numbers can never both win, and $\Pwin = 2p \approx 0.2118$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  In the canonical partition of Definition~\ref{def:partition}, both
  tickets winning means $X_0 + X_1 \ge t$ and $X_0 + X_2 \ge t$.  Adding,
  $2X_0 + X_1 + X_2 \ge 2t$.  Since $X_0 + X_1 + X_2 = k - X_3 \le k$,
  this forces $X_0 \ge 2t - k + X_3 \ge 2t - k$.  But
  $X_0 \le |S_0| = m \le 2t-k-1$, a contradiction.  Hence no outcome with
  both tickets winning exists and $g(m) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Negative correlation is advantageous]
  At $m = m^*$, the covariance formula \eqref{eq:cov} gives
  $\mathrm{Cov}(|A_1 \cap R|, |A_2 \cap R|) = -1.0 < 0$.
  Intuitively, if $A_1$ scores
  poorly because few elements of its unique region $S_1$ are drawn, that
  means more draws fall in $S_2$ (since $|S_3|=0$ at $m=m^*$ for the
  Lotofácil), boosting $A_2$'s score.  This negative correlation is
  precisely what makes minimum-overlap collections effective.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Three and more tickets: exact structure and the limits of
            monotonicity}
\label{sec:general-n}

For $n \ge 3$ tickets, inclusion--exclusion introduces higher-order joint
probabilities, and two phenomena appear that the two-ticket analysis cannot
see.  First, $\Pwin$ is no longer a function of the pairwise overlaps
alone.  Second, the natural extension of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-ticket-monotone} --- ``decreasing any single pairwise
overlap, all other pairs held fixed, cannot decrease $\Pwin$; hence the
optimum has all pairwise overlaps equal to $m^*$'' --- is \emph{false},
both because the all-$m^*$ profile is combinatorially infeasible for
$n \ge 3$ and because coordinatewise monotonicity itself fails between
realisable collections.  This subsection establishes the complete
picture: monotonicity survives at $n = 3$ at the level of best
realisations (Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-monotone}) and fails from $n = 4$
onwards (Example~\ref{ex:n4-counterexample}), while the design principle
that emerges --- balanced profiles at the degree bound --- is what
persists for every $n$.

Associate with a triple $(A_1,A_2,A_3)$ its \emph{intersection profile}:
the pairwise overlaps $(m_{12}, m_{13}, m_{23})$ together with the triple
intersection $c = |A_1 \cap A_2 \cap A_3|$.  The sizes of the eight Venn
cells of the triple are determined by the profile, the distribution of the
draw over the cells is multivariate hypergeometric, and $\Pwin$ depends on
the collection only through the profile.

\begin{proposition}[Exact three-ticket decomposition]
  \label{prop:three-ticket}
  For any triple of tickets,
  \[
    \Pwin(A_1,A_2,A_3)
    = 3\pone - \bigl[g(m_{12}) + g(m_{13}) + g(m_{23})\bigr] + g_3,
  \]
  where $g_3 = P(\text{all three tickets win})$.  Moreover, $g_3 = 0$
  whenever $\min_{i<j} m_{ij} \le 2t-k-1$.  In general $g_3$ depends on
  the triple intersection $c$, not only on the pairwise overlaps.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The identity is inclusion--exclusion for three events, with the
  pairwise terms $P(E_i \cap E_j) = g(m_{ij})$ from \eqref{eq:ie}.  For
  the second claim, the triple-win event is contained in each pairwise
  joint-win event, and the latter has probability zero when
  $m_{ij} \le 2t-k-1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:mutual-excl}).
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Pairwise overlaps do not determine $\Pwin$]
  \label{ex:ill-posed}
  For the Lotofácil profile $(m_{12},m_{13},m_{23}) = (14,14,14)$ the
  triple intersection can be $c = 13$ or $c = 14$, and exact enumeration
  gives $\Pwin = 0.172035$ and $\Pwin = 0.165911$, respectively.  Among
  the $1{,}250$ feasible Lotofácil triple profiles, $235$
  pairwise-overlap vectors admit more than one value of $\Pwin$.  The map
  $(m_{12},m_{13},m_{23}) \mapsto \Pwin$ is therefore not well defined for
  $n \ge 3$: any monotonicity statement must either fix the full
  intersection profile or quantify over realisations.
\end{example}

The second obstruction is combinatorial.

\begin{proposition}[Degree-counting lower bound on total overlap]
  \label{prop:degree-bound}
  For a collection of $n$ tickets let $d_x = \#\{i : x \in A_i\}$ for
  $x \in [v]$.  Then
  \[
    \Omega(\mathcal{A}) \;=\; \sum_{i<j} m_{ij}
    \;=\; \sum_{x \in [v]} \binom{d_x}{2}
    \;\ge\; r \binom{q+1}{2} + (v - r)\binom{q}{2},
  \]
  where $nk = qv + r$ with $0 \le r < v$.  For the Lotofácil and $n = 3$
  this gives $\Omega \ge 20 > 15 = \binom{3}{2}\, m^*$: \emph{no} three
  tickets have all pairwise overlaps equal to $m^* = 5$, and at least one
  pair must share at least $\lceil 20/3 \rceil = 7$ numbers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Both sides of the identity count incidences: an element $x$ contained in
  $d_x$ tickets contributes $\binom{d_x}{2}$ to the sum of pairwise
  overlaps.  The lower bound is the minimum of $\sum_x \binom{d_x}{2}$
  over integer degree sequences with $\sum_x d_x = nk$, attained by the
  most balanced sequence ($r$ elements of degree $q+1$, $v-r$ of degree
  $q$), by convexity of $u \mapsto \binom{u}{2}$.
\end{proof}

Combining the decomposition with the feasibility bound yields the exact
optimum for three Lotofácil tickets --- and it is \emph{not} a
minimum-overlap configuration.

\begin{theorem}[Exact optimum for three Lotofácil tickets]
  \label{thm:three-optimal}
  For $(v,k,t) = (25,15,11)$ and $n = 3$,
  \[
    \max_{\mathcal{A}} \Pwin(\mathcal{A})
    \;=\; 3\pone - 2\,g(7)
    \;=\; \frac{1{,}028{,}578}{3{,}268{,}760}
    \;\approx\; 0.314669,
  \]
  attained exactly by the collections with pairwise overlap profile
  $\{7,7,6\}$, for which the triple intersection is forced to be empty.
  The optimal profile is \emph{balanced}: it contains no pair at the
  minimum overlap $m^* = 5$.  Indeed, any collection in which some pair
  attains $m^*$ satisfies
  $\Pwin \le 3\pone - g(7) - g(8) \approx 0.311296$, strictly below the
  optimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By exhaustive enumeration of all $1{,}250$ feasible intersection
  profiles, each evaluated exactly via
  Proposition~\ref{prop:three-ticket}, with $g_3$ computed by enumeration
  of the multivariate hypergeometric distribution over the eight Venn
  cells (verification scripts in the supplementary material).  The
  mechanism is transparent.  Profiles with $\Omega = 20$ attain the
  degree bound, and each of them contains a pair with overlap at most
  $6$, forcing $g_3 = 0$ and
  $\Pwin = 3\pone - \sum_{i<j} g(m_{ij})$.  Since $g$ vanishes up to
  overlap $6$ and its increments are increasing beyond it
  (Table~\ref{tab:delta-g}), the loss $\sum_{i<j} g(m_{ij})$ is minimised
  by the most balanced profile $\{7,7,6\}$, which pays only $2g(7)
  \approx 0.0030$; the extreme profile $\{5,5,10\}$ pays $g(10) \approx
  0.0167$.  Profiles with $\Omega \ge 21$ cannot compensate through
  $g_3$, as the enumeration confirms.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
  Write the inclusion--exclusion expansion
  $\Pwin \binom{v}{k}
   = \sum_{\emptyset \ne U \subseteq [n]} (-1)^{|U|+1} J(U)$,
  where $J(U)$ counts the draws on which every ticket in $U$ wins.
  Truncating after the third-order terms gives the Bonferroni upper bound
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:bonferroni-ub}
    \Pwin \;\le\; n\pone - \sum_{\text{pairs}} g(m_{ij})
      + \sum_{\text{triples}}
        g_3^{\max}(m_{ab}, m_{ac}, m_{bc}),
  \end{equation}
  where $g_3^{\max}$ maximises the exact triple-win probability over the
  triple intersection; the bound is legitimate because each embedded
  triple is standalone-feasible.  A depth-first search over the profiles
  in $\{5,\ldots,15\}^{\binom{n}{2}}$ with
  $\sum_{i<j} m_{ij} \ge \Omega_{\min}$
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-bound}, with $\Omega_{\min} = 45, 75, 120$
  for $n = 4,5,6$), pruned by \eqref{eq:bonferroni-ub} against the
  incumbent value, leaves $1$, $7$ and $1{,}119$ profiles up to
  relabelling respectively, having visited $2.6 \times 10^4$,
  $1.9 \times 10^7$ and $2.8 \times 10^{11}$ nodes.  For each surviving
  profile, every admissible intersection structure --- that is, every
  assignment of the higher-order intersections $|A_{i_1} \cap \cdots \cap
  A_{i_\ell}|$, $\ell \ge 3$, whose induced Venn cell sizes are all
  non-negative, so that the structure is realisable --- is enumerated and
  evaluated exactly, and the maximum equals the value in
  Table~\ref{tab:certified-optima}, which is attained in each case by
  collections that Algorithm~\ref{alg:sa} produces
  (Section~\ref{sec:empirical}).  Every step uses exact
  integer arithmetic; the certified optimal collection is cross-validated
  independently by brute force over all $\binom{25}{15} = 3{,}268{,}760$
  draws.  The searches take seconds for $n = 4, 5$ and $13.5$ hours on
  ten cores for $n = 6$; the code is in the supplementary material.
\end{proof}

The certificates also describe the optimal collections, and the
description degrades in an instructive way as $n$ grows.

\begin{remark}[Structure of the certified optima]
  \label{rem:optimal-structure}
  At all four certified optima the inclusion--exclusion expansion
  terminates after the third order:
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:opt-decomposition}
    \Pwin
    \;=\; n\pone - \sum_{i<j} g(m_{ij}) + \sum_{a<b<c} g_3(A_a,A_b,A_c),
  \end{equation}
  every fourth- and higher-order term being zero.  All the quantities
  involved are integer multiples of $1/\binom{25}{15}$; we quote the
  corresponding integers below.  In each case the degree sequence is
  forced by Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-bound}: $(3^{10}, 2^{15})$ for
  $n = 4$, $(3^{25})$ for $n = 5$ and $(4^{15}, 3^{10})$ for $n = 6$.
  The optima then differ in how much third-order mass they can carry.
  Write $T_{abc} = |A_a \cap A_b \cap A_c|$.  For $n = 4$ the unique
  triple whose three pairwise overlaps equal $8$ has $T_{abc} = 4$ and
  contributes $160$, the four-way intersection being empty; for $n = 5$
  the five triples carrying two of the five $8$-overlaps have $T_{abc} =
  3$ and contribute $25$ each, for a total of $125$, all higher
  intersections being empty.  For $n = 6$, the identity $\sum_{a<b<c}
  T_{abc} = \sum_x \binom{d_x}{3} = 70$ over the $20$ triples, together
  with the values $0, 0, 125, 160$ of the triple-win count at
  $m_{ab}=m_{ac}=m_{bc}=8$ and $T_{abc} = 1,2,3,4$, makes the third-order
  sum maximal exactly when ten triples have $T_{abc} = 4$ and ten have
  $T_{abc} = 3$, giving $2{,}850$.

  A consequence is that optimality pins down the collection to different
  degrees.  For $n = 4$ and $n = 5$ the optimal profile admits a
  \emph{unique} admissible intersection structure, so the certificate
  determines the optimal collection completely up to relabelling the
  tickets and permuting $[v]$.  For $n = 6$ the profile $\{8^{15}\}$ is
  still uniquely optimal, but exactly $954$ admissible intersection
  structures attain the maximum, since only the \emph{counts} of triples
  at $T = 3$ and $T = 4$ enter \eqref{eq:opt-decomposition}, not their
  arrangement.  Beyond $n = 5$, therefore, it is the overlap profile ---
  not the full intersection pattern --- that optimality determines.
\end{remark}

We now show that the naive coordinatewise-monotonicity extension fails
outright, even in the regime $t > k/2$ of interest here.

\begin{example}[Coordinatewise monotonicity fails for $n = 3$]
  \label{ex:counterexample}
  Let $(v,k,t) = (11,7,4)$, so $m^* = 3$ and $t > k/2$.  Consider the two
  collections
  \begin{align*}
    \mathcal{A}:\quad
      & A_1 = \{1,2,3,4,5\} \cup \{6,7\}, \quad
        A_2 = \{1,2,3,4,5\} \cup \{8,9\}, \\
      & A_3 = \{1,2,3,4,5\} \cup \{10,11\}; \\[2pt]
    \mathcal{B}:\quad
      & B_1 = \{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}, \quad
        B_2 = \{1,2,3,4,5,6,8\}, \quad
        B_3 = \{1,2,3,4,7,8,9\}.
  \end{align*}
  $\mathcal{A}$ has pairwise overlaps $(5,5,5)$ and triple intersection
  $5$; $\mathcal{B}$ has overlaps $(6,5,5)$ and triple intersection $4$:
  the overlap $m_{12}$ \emph{increased} by one while $m_{13} = m_{23} = 5$
  are unchanged.  Enumerating all $\binom{11}{7} = 330$ draws,
  \[
    \Pwin(\mathcal{A}) = \tfrac{325}{330}
    \;<\; \tfrac{329}{330} = \Pwin(\mathcal{B}).
  \]
  Increasing a pairwise overlap strictly increased the joint prize
  probability.  The explanation lies in the third-order structure that
  Example~\ref{ex:ill-posed} shows to matter: the trade was one unit of
  pairwise overlap for one unit of triple intersection, and the latter is
  more damaging.  Exhaustive search finds analogous strict counterexamples
  in six parameter families $(v,k,t)$ with $v \le 11$.
\end{example}

For the Lotofácil parameters themselves no such counterexample exists at
$n = 3$: over all $1{,}250$ feasible profiles there is no pair of
collections whose overlap vectors differ by $+1$ in one coordinate and
whose $\Pwin$ values are ordered the wrong way.  This motivates studying
monotonicity at the level of \emph{best realisations}: for a feasible
pairwise-overlap vector $M = (m_{ij})_{1 \le i < j \le n}$ define
\[
  F(M) \;=\; \max\bigl\{\Pwin(\mathcal{A}) :
    \mathcal{A} \in \tbinom{[v]}{k}^n \text{ realises } M\bigr\}.
\]
For $n = 3$ this restores the monotone picture completely, and the proof
is a two-step argument built on a generalisation of the swap coupling of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-ticket-monotone}.

\begin{lemma}[Pattern-preserving swap monotonicity]
  \label{lem:pattern-swap}
  Let $n \ge 2$ and $A_1, \ldots, A_n \in \binom{[v]}{k}$.  Suppose
  $x \in A_1 \setminus A_2$ and $y \in A_2 \setminus A_1$ satisfy
  $\ind{x \in A_j} = \ind{y \in A_j}$ for every $j \ge 3$, and set
  $A_2' = (A_2 \setminus \{y\}) \cup \{x\}$, so that
  $|A_1 \cap A_2'| = |A_1 \cap A_2| + 1$ while every other pairwise
  overlap is unchanged.  Writing $S_j = |A_j \cap R|$ and
  $W = |A_2 \cap R|$,
  \begin{align*}
    &\Pwin(A_1, A_2', A_3, \ldots, A_n)
      - \Pwin(A_1, A_2, A_3, \ldots, A_n) \\
    &\qquad = -\,P\bigl(S_1 = t,\; W = t-1,\;
        S_j \le t-1 \;\forall j \ge 3,\;
        x \in R,\; y \notin R\bigr)
      \;\le\; 0.
  \end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Repeat the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-ticket-monotone} with the
  failure event $\bigcap_{j \ne 2}\{S_j < t\}$ carried along.
  Decomposing by $(\ind{x \in R}, \ind{y \in R})$, the equal cases cancel
  and
  \[
    P(\text{all fail w.r.t.\ } A_2') - P(\text{all fail w.r.t.\ } A_2)
    = P\bigl(D,\, W = t\bigr) - P\bigl(D',\, W = t-1\bigr),
  \]
  where $D = \{S_1 < t,\, S_j < t \;\forall j \ge 3,\, x \notin R,\,
  y \in R\}$ and $D' = \{S_1 < t,\, S_j < t \;\forall j \ge 3,\,
  x \in R,\, y \notin R\}$.  The swap map
  $\varphi(R) = (R \setminus \{y\}) \cup \{x\}$ increases $S_1$ by one,
  decreases $W$ by one, and --- by the membership hypothesis on $x, y$ ---
  leaves every $S_j$ with $j \ge 3$ unchanged.  Hence it maps the first
  event bijectively onto $\{S_1 \le t,\, S_j < t \;\forall j \ge 3,\,
  W = t-1,\, x \in R,\, y \notin R\}$, and the difference of failure
  probabilities collapses to
  $P(S_1 = t,\, W = t-1,\, S_j < t \;\forall j \ge 3,\, x \in R,\,
  y \notin R) \ge 0$.  The claim follows by complementation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Profile monotonicity for three tickets]
  \label{thm:profile-monotone}
  Fix any $(v,k,t)$ and $n = 3$.  If $M$ and $M + e_{ij}$ are both
  feasible overlap vectors, then $F(M) \ge F(M + e_{ij})$: the
  best-realisation value function is non-increasing in each coordinate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Without loss of generality $(i,j) = (1,2)$.  Let
  $\mathcal{A}' = (A_1, A_2, A_3)$ attain $F(M + e_{12})$, with Venn cells
  $(c_{123}, c_{12}, c_{13}, c_{23}, c_1, c_2, c_3, c_0)$.  If there
  exist $z \in A_1 \cap A_2$ and $w \notin A_1 \cup A_2$ with
  $\ind{z \in A_3} = \ind{w \in A_3}$, then
  $\mathcal{A} = (A_1, (A_2 \setminus \{z\}) \cup \{w\}, A_3)$ realises
  $M$, and $\mathcal{A}'$ is recovered from $\mathcal{A}$ by a
  pattern-preserving swap; Lemma~\ref{lem:pattern-swap} then gives
  $F(M) \ge \Pwin(\mathcal{A}) \ge \Pwin(\mathcal{A}') = F(M + e_{12})$.
  Such a pair $(z, w)$ exists whenever
  $(c_{123} \ge 1 \wedge c_3 \ge 1)$ or
  $(c_{12} \ge 1 \wedge c_0 \ge 1)$.  Suppose both fail, i.e.\
  $(c_{123} = 0 \vee c_3 = 0) \wedge (c_{12} = 0 \vee c_0 = 0)$; since
  $c_{123} + c_{12} = m_{12} + 1 \ge 1$ rules out
  $c_{123} = c_{12} = 0$, three cases remain, and each contradicts the
  feasibility of $M$:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item[(a)] $c_{123} = 0$ and $c_0 = 0$: then
      $|A_1 \cup A_2 \cup A_3| = 3k - (m_{12}{+}1) - m_{13} - m_{23} = v$,
      so any realisation of $M$ would have union of size at least
      $3k - m_{12} - m_{13} - m_{23} = v + 1 > v$.
    \item[(b)] $c_{12} = 0$ and $c_3 = 0$: then $c_{123} = m_{12}+1$ and
      $c_3 = k - m_{13} - m_{23} + c_{123} = 0$ give
      $m_{13} + m_{23} = k + m_{12} + 1$; any realisation of $M$ needs
      triple intersection $c \ge m_{13} + m_{23} - k = m_{12} + 1$, yet
      $c \le m_{12}$.
    \item[(c)] $c_3 = 0$ and $c_0 = 0$: then
      $[v] = A_1 \cup A_2 \cup A_3 = A_1 \cup A_2$, so
      $v = 2k - (m_{12}+1)$, i.e.\ $m_{12} + 1 = 2k - v \le m^*$; but $M$
      feasible requires $m_{12} \ge m^*$.
  \end{itemize}
  Hence when $M$ is feasible the descent always exists, proving the
  theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sharpness in both directions]
  Theorem~\ref{thm:profile-monotone} is sharp in two senses.  First, the
  maximum over realisations is essential: the realisation-wise statement
  fails (Example~\ref{ex:counterexample}).  Second, the restriction to
  $n = 3$ is essential, as the next example shows.  Exhaustive exact
  verification for all $(v,k,t)$ with $v \le 13$ corroborates the theorem
  (supplementary scripts).
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Profile monotonicity fails for $n = 4$]
  \label{ex:n4-counterexample}
  Let $(v,k,t) = (9,3,2)$ (note $t > k/2$) and $n = 4$.  Exhaustive
  enumeration of \emph{all} four-ticket collections shows that the best
  realisation of the profile $(2,2,2,2,2,1)$ achieves
  $F = 42/84$, while the \emph{sunflower} collection
  \[
    \{1,2,3\},\; \{1,2,4\},\; \{1,2,5\},\; \{1,2,6\}
  \]
  realises the coordinatewise-\emph{larger} profile $(2,2,2,2,2,2)$ with
  $\Pwin = 43/84$.  Hence $F(2,2,2,2,2,1) < F(2,2,2,2,2,2)$: increasing a
  pairwise overlap increases the best attainable winning probability.
  Analogous violations occur for $(7,3,2)$, $(8,3,2)$ and $(9,4,3)$
  (the latter also with $t > k/2$).  Best-realisation monotonicity is
  therefore a genuinely low-$n$ phenomenon: it holds for $n \in \{2,3\}$
  and fails from $n = 4$ onwards.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[What survives for $n \ge 4$]
  \label{rem:balance-principle}
  Two structural facts persist for every $n$.  First,
  Lemma~\ref{lem:pattern-swap} holds for all $n$: along any swap that
  preserves the membership pattern in the remaining tickets, $\Pwin$ can
  only decrease.  Second, the counterexamples do not disturb the
  \emph{optima}: in every instance where we can certify the global
  optimum --- the Lotofácil with $3 \le n \le 6$
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:three-optimal} and~\ref{thm:certified-optima}), and the
  families $(9,3,2)$ and $(9,4,3)$ with $n = 4$, whose global optima are
  the profiles $(0,0,0,1,1,1)$ with $\Pwin = 65/84$ and $(1,1,1,1,1,2)$
  with $\Pwin = 80/126$ --- the optimal collection attains the degree bound of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:degree-bound} with the most balanced admissible
  profile, exactly as the second-order surrogate $\sum_{i<j} g(m_{ij})$
  predicts.  (The monotonicity violations occur in suboptimal regions of
  the profile landscape, such as the sunflower family above.)  Whether a
  global optimum always attains the degree bound with a
  $\sum g$-minimising profile is, to our knowledge, open; we leave it as
  the natural successor to the monotonicity question.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Consequence for algorithm design]
  \label{rem:objective}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:three-optimal} shows that minimising the total overlap
  $\Omega(\mathcal{A})$ is the wrong surrogate objective: all
  $\Omega = 20$ profiles are tied under $\Omega$, yet their $\Pwin$ values
  span $0.3010$--$0.3147$.  The correct second-order surrogate is
  \[
    G(\mathcal{A}) \;=\; \sum_{i<j} g(m_{ij}),
  \]
  which by inclusion--exclusion captures $1 - \Pwin$ up to higher-order
  joint-win terms (nonnegative and typically much smaller).  Minimising
  $G$ automatically produces balanced profiles.
  Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} adopts this objective for the simulated
  annealing stage, and Section~\ref{sec:empirical} quantifies the
  resulting gains.
\end{remark}
